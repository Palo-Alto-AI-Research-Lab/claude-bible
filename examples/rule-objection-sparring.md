# Rule — verdict = objection list + "convince me"

> Sanitized from our live system. Born the same day a real no-go verdict was overturned: the agent's research said "don't productize" with 14 objections; the owner's rebuttal reframed the question ("we're not selling a product, we're giving away a free school") and 13 of 14 objections dissolved in one pass. The rule captures the mechanism so it fires every time.

## Why
The principal often sees better: they hold context and framing the agent lacks. A "no-go" delivered as a sentence buries information that only a debate can extract. The objection list works as a questionnaire; the rebuttal is new data; the synthesis beats what either side had alone.

## The rule
1. **Trigger:** the agent concludes "don't / not yet / no-go" on something the principal wants — or the principal asks "give me your objections".
2. **Format:** deliver the verdict as a **numbered list of objections**, one real objection per line, not buried in prose. Then explicitly invite: "here are my objections — convince me."
3. **Listen for the frame, not just the counterarguments.** Rebuttals most often win by changing what question is being asked.
4. **Synthesize:** a table of objection → dissolved (why) / stands. What stands is recorded as a boundary INSIDE the new decision — kept, not lost.
5. **Overturned verdict = a real decision:** new memo, `supersedes` on the old one, an OVERTURN mark in the declined-decisions journal, so no parallel session cites the dead verdict as live.
6. **Anti-sycophancy:** objections must be genuine and strong, dissolution must be argued. If a rebuttal didn't close an objection, say so plainly. Consensus = an honest deadlock of mutual persuasion, not capitulation.
7. **Symmetric:** when the principal rejects the agent's idea, the agent may ask for the objections as a list and work them — respecting the declined journal (no re-pitch without a new argument).

## Boundary
Does not weaken hard gates: money, irreversible actions, publishing, secrets still require the principal's explicit approval regardless of who won the debate.
