## Decision chain (how we got here)
1. Deep research round A on public productization returned NO-GO: saturated PKM market, single-tenant architecture, trust/liability of a live messaging agent, solo-support burden.
2. The no-go was delivered per our house rule as 14 numbered objections + an invitation to rebut.
3. The rebuttal reframed the question: not a paid product/SaaS, but a free giveaway + education, self-hosted (every user runs their own single-tenant copy; we host nothing). 13/14 objections dissolved under the new frame; the survivors became invariants: leak-scan before every publication, owner reviews every first push, slice by value.
4. Verdict overturned and recorded properly: new decision supersedes the old memo; the old entry got an OVERTURN mark in the declined-decisions journal so parallel agent sessions can't cite the dead verdict.
5. Flagship choice criterion was "most popular + simplest": the Bible framework won (pure markdown, zero dependencies, relevant to anyone whose CLAUDE.md is bloating). Runner-ups queued: multi-machine consensus protocol, CRM-to-messaging personalization.
6. Repo drafted in a local staging dir, deterministic leak-scan over all files (names, chat IDs, key patterns, paths: zero hits), pushed with a noreply commit identity.

## Deep-research inputs (rounds referenced)
- Round A: productization go/no-go - market saturation, retention (~30-40% at 1 month for PKM+AI tools), open-core/SaaS precedents (memory layers, workflow builders), solo-founder support limits.
- Round B: distribution channels - official curated marketplace vs community marketplaces vs auto-indexing directories vs awesome-lists (social-proof gate ~10 stars). Plan: GitHub → auto-indexed directories → awesome-list after first stars.
